text on surface  17.42:1 link on surface  6.99:1 hover on surface  8.71:1

Every pair is measured before it ships, with the ratio in a comment beside the token. AA asks 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for the rest. Links are underlined as well as colored.

Retired. indianred at 3.98:1, steelblue at 4.11:1, olive at 4.20:1, all under the body-text minimum. No page overrides them now.

Type

Roboto Slab for headings, Roboto for the rest, a 1.25 major third off a 17px base. Both are self-hosted variable files with font-display: swap, so nothing loads from another origin.

--text-4xl / slab 700 Steve here.

--text-2xl / slab 700 Weird, pretty things

--text-lg / sans 300 The lede under a page title, one size up from the body.

label / sans 700, .18em Selected Work

--text-base / sans 300 Body runs 300 at a 65ch measure. Every column sets ragged. Justification opens rivers without hyphenation, and it reads worse for dyslexia.

--font-mono measured values, ratios, readouts, filenames

Labels and mono captions sit at full ink. Nothing in the chrome drops under 10px or under the body-text minimum.

The Motion Rules

Reduced motion wins
prefers-reduced-motion: reduce halts every animation, including the ones declared inside a page's own style block. The guard lives in css/site.css.
Three flashes a second
Nothing flashes more than three times a second. The strobing work is built to that line.
A way to stop it
Motion running longer than five seconds carries a pause control.
Decorative motion serves reading
Hover, focus, and state changes take --duration-fast and --ease-out.

The Shell

A skip link first in the tab order, then the masthead: nav flush left, ב״ה alone in the right corner. Then one <main>, one h1, and the outbound links at the foot. No boxes and no rules: margin and gutter carry the structure.

One main, one h1
Content pages wrap the body in <main class="shell" id="main">. The art pages make the stage the main, so the art is the content.
Jump links land
Every jump target carries an id and tabindex="-1", and js/jump.js moves the focus to match the scroll. A new in-page link needs no code beyond the href.
Relative links
Links read work/ and ../, never /work/. A root-relative href dies over file://, so the folder browses with no server.
One head block
Per-page title, description, and canonical; the same author, theme-color pair, Open Graph set, card, and icons everywhere. One share card for the site.

The Wordmark

Whole

Cropped, under 768px

The signature is the only mark, and it inherits currentColor. It sits in the homepage body rather than the masthead. Shown whole, it wants room: about 180px or wider, with clear space near the height of its capitals. No photograph under it, and no shadow.

Cropping allowed. On mobile, the homepage scales the mark far past its frame and rotates it for abstract texture, just like /logo. Stroke scales with the mark.

The Square Control

A 56px square, a 2px rule, a mark, and a caption that never hides. The caption is the accessible name. A toggle fills solid and renames itself, so state never rides on the fill. Under 600px the square drops to 48px, over the 44px floor.

The Marks

  • Play, and two bars while it runs. The only mark that changes shape, because it is the only one holding a state. /logo, /tartan, /larvascript
  • & Steps the work itself. The track on the logo page, the weave on the tartan, the scene in Larvascript. /logo, /tartan, /larvascript

  • § The hue on the tartan, the source picture in Larvascript. /tartan, /larvascript

A mark means what its page needs. The one rule: never two of the same on a page. A mark with no state to show answers a press by turning once.

The Bar

The bar carries the h1, a readout naming what is showing, and the controls. Controls hug the right edge, one gap between them, each matching the width of its square.

The readout is a polite live region in mono. The eye reads Drift / Plain; a screen reader hears "Scene: Drift, Effect: Plain."

Step, never shuffle.

Every set steps forward and wraps, so a visitor who overshoots keeps pressing and gets back. Nobody arrives wanting the hunting tartan by name, so no piece gets a row of switches. Random survives in one place: Atextual opens on a random page and reads forward.

Chrome Away

Clicking the art hides the head and the bar, and Escape does the same. A corner hint reads "Click art or press Esc to restore controls". The art is the button: a transparent hit target over the stage, named for screen readers.

Going bare moves the focus to that target, and the stage draws its ring. Every piece but Atextual, which has no picture to clear.

The Motion Gate

Larvascript strobes on purpose, so nobody lands in it. The page opens on a still picture, and the warning arrives the first time a visitor asks for motion. Either answer closes it for the visit.

A <dialog> on showModal(): the browser traps the keyboard, makes the page inert, answers Escape, and reports a modal. None of it rebuilt out of ARIA.

Ask for reduced motion and the warning gets longer, with the still answer taking the fill and the focus.

Where a piece cannot be made safe without destroying it, say so, offer the still version, and let the visitor choose.

Focus and the Floor

One ring
A 3px accent outline at a 3px offset, over 3:1 on both surfaces. The only page that redraws it is a piece with a clipping stage.
Semantic first
If an element does the job, use the element. ARIA second, with the reason in a comment. A name that flips, Play to Pause, carries its own state and skips aria-pressed.
Names, never labels on prose
aria-label never goes on a paragraph, a div, or a span, where ARIA prohibits naming. The words go in a .sr-only span. An SVG acting as a picture takes role="img".
Headings and links
One h1 a page, no skipped levels, and link text that names its destination. Never here, click here, or read more.
Everything else
Alt text on every image, or decorative on purpose. Captions or a text alternative wherever there is narration. A name on every control. Color never alone. lang on the html element and on the Hebrew. The keyboard reaching everything the mouse reaches.

Words

Short sentences, specific images, eighth grade words, and no sentence ending on a preposition.

Titles
Title case, unless the title is a sentence. Then sentence case, and it keeps its period. "Plaid to the Bone" and "Weird, pretty things."
Captions
One word on the screen. The verb is screen reader text inside it.
Alt text
Describes the content, never the filename: "navy plaid tipped onto the diagonal, its red and green bands broken into running dashes."
Counts
Name the set, never its size. A figure goes stale the day the set changes, so the site says "my art."
Dashes
No em dash, no en dash, and no pair of hyphens dressed as one. Rewrite the sentence. An ellipsis takes a leading space …
